Chapter 1

Start here

By November 2026 the system hadn’t collapsed. It had hollowed out. The headline structures survive, but their binding parts (the 2050 dates, civil liability, hourly matching, financed-emissions targets) were removed or deferred, and one tonne no longer means the same thing in law, targets, markets and marketing.

Every number in this guide links to its source; press a number to see it. Open the six findings below, or follow the chapters in order.

Of the Commission’s €6.3bn a year in Omnibus savings, €4.9bn comes from cutting companies out of scope and only about €0.5bn from simpler standards . The revised ESRS cut mandatory datapoints by 61%, but preparers expect only about 20% lower recurring cost .

Only 12% of companies supported hourly matching , and in April 2026 more than 48 companies including Apple and Amazon publicly asked for the rules to be optional . In October the GHG Protocol let the 2015 method continue until an undetermined date .

A CSRD reporter with an SBTi target and RE100 membership can owe location-based, two market-based, an SBTi electricity percentage, an RE100 percentage and a proposed impact figure. Each is legitimate under its own rulebook; none reconciles to another. Try the builder in chapter 7.

Methodology, quality label, ratings, host authorisation, corresponding adjustment, claims code and legal review. They don’t nest, so a credit can carry the ICVCM label yet be ineligible for CORSIA . CORSIA’s first phase needs 100–250M units; only about 33–38M had cleared every gate .

Registries for SAF, steel and shipping certificates are trading now, but the GHG Protocol won’t publish its consolidated standard until Q4 2028, and it currently bars market-based Scope 3 from the inventory . SBTi V2 recognises commodity certificates from February 2027 .

Voluntary biodiversity credits have sold about $6.8m since 2022 , against a $942bn a year biodiversity finance gap . None of the four biodiversity standards assessed by B4B+ reached its high-integrity threshold , and they sit outside both the new US accounting standard and UNIDROIT’s draft property principles .

    Chapter 3

    The mandatory rules shrank by scope, not by simplification

    The EU cut who has to report far more than what they report. The US federal layer retreated while states built their own. The ISSB “global baseline” became a menu.

    The EU Omnibus CSRD, CSDDD, ESRS

    Directive (EU) 2026/470 was published on 26 February 2026 and took effect on 18 March . CSRD now covers EU companies with more than 1,000 employees and €450m turnover from FY2027; CSDDD covers more than 5,000 employees and €1.5bn from 26 July 2029, with EU-wide civil liability and the climate-transition-plan duty deleted and penalties capped at 3% of turnover . The original CSRD would have covered about 50,000 companies .

    The revised ESRS were adopted on 3 July, published as Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1563 on 21 September, and came into force on 10 November 2026 . For FY2026 companies can stay on the old ESRS (with or without new reliefs) or adopt the revised set early, as long as they say which . Comparability, the point of mandatory reporting, is degraded during the transition by design.

    The sequence also shows the whiplash: a “stop-the-clock” delay in 2025, a Parliament position in November 2025 that would have set a 1,750-employee threshold, a final vote of 428–218–17 in December, then the final law . That is two enacted scope regimes and one proposed in 18 months.

    What it costs and the figure everyone quotes

    EFRAG puts first-year internal ESRS costs at €130k for preparers under 10,000 employees and €540k above that, plus €40k–390k of consultancy; survey medians were about €500k internal and €300k external. The 2020 impact assessment had assumed €128,848 a year .

    That 3–7 times gap, more than any datapoint count, fuelled the “bureaucracy monster” politics . Whether €0.8m is a lot depends on what you divide it by; chapter 8 has a calculator.

    Carbon pricing and product rules CBAM, ETS2, EUDR, green claims

    CBAM is the one clean example of simplification that kept coverage: the 50-tonne threshold exempts about 90% of roughly 200,000 importers while keeping 99% of embedded emissions, by the Commission’s estimate . Certificate sales start on 1 February 2027 , and the UK’s own CBAM starts on 1 January 2027 with no link to the EU scheme yet .

    ETS2 was pushed to 2028 . The deforestation regulation was delayed twice, to 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators . The EU ban on offset-based product neutrality claims has applied since 27 September 2026 , while the broader Green Claims Directive is listed for withdrawal in the Commission’s 2027 work programme and remains formally pending .

    The United States SEC, California, EPA, anti-ESG

    The SEC rule adopted in March 2024 never took effect; the SEC proposed full rescission on 29 May 2026, estimating compliance at $197,000 to over $739,000 per registrant per year . No final rescission had been adopted by 10 November .

    California went ahead, erratically: CARB’s SB 253 regulation took effect on 12 October, four weeks before the first Scope 1/2 filing deadline of 10 November . On 9 November the Ninth Circuit let Scope 1/2 reporting proceed as commercial speech, but found SB 253’s Scope 3 requirement and all of SB 261 likely unconstitutional; one judge would have applied strict scrutiny to both laws .

    EPA rescinded the endangerment finding effective 20 April 2026 and moved the reporting-year 2025 GHGRP deadline to 10 March 2027 without notice and comment . Meanwhile Texas’s anti-ESG law SB13 was struck down , and in Texas v. BlackRock the climate commitments that EU and ISSB-aligned regimes encourage are pleaded as a cartel .

    The ISSB “global baseline” 35 jurisdictions, many versions

    S&P Global counts 35 jurisdictions adopting IFRS S1/S2 in some form . Australia, Hong Kong and Taiwan mandate only the climate standard; Japan and Korea mandate both; the UK puts S1 and Scope 3 on comply-or-explain . The ISSB itself added jurisdictional reliefs on warming potentials and the GHG Protocol from 2027 . Brazil moved to comply-or-explain in June 2026 and Singapore deferred .

    A survey of 92 multinationals counts more than 600 reporting provisions worldwide; 57% spend over $1m a year and BASF $10–15m .

    Who drove the rollback

    The Draghi report framed reporting as a top burden ; a centre-right and far-right majority passed Parliament’s position ; and paragraph 12 of the August 2025 US–EU trade deal committed the EU to revisit exactly the CSDDD provisions later deleted . ExxonMobil held at least 25 meetings with EU institutions , and Corporate Europe Observatory counts 84% of Omnibus-related Commissioner meetings with business ◆advocacy source.

    Voluntary standards were supposed to steady the mandatory ones. Instead they moved faster, and most of their new effective dates land around 1 January 2027.

    The GHG Protocol merges with ISO

    The 2004 Corporate Standard, 2011 Scope 3 Standard and 2015 Scope 2 Guidance are being merged with ISO 14064-1 into one co-branded standard: draft for consultation in Q2 2027, final in Q4 2028, with no effective dates set . In July 2025 the plan had been final Scope 2 text by mid-2026 . Proposals on the table include making Scope 3 mandatory with 95% coverage and a new Category 16 for facilitated activities .

    SBTi’s turbulent two years

    In March 2024, 239 companies including Microsoft, Unilever and Walmart were moved to “commitment removed” ; by mid-2025, 93% of them still had no validated net-zero target . In April 2024 the board announced it would allow certificates and credits for Scope 3, then reversed within a week after a staff revolt . V2.0, published 11 June 2026, takes effect 1 February 2027 .

    Everything else that moved

    CDP merged its questionnaires and warns that methodology changes yearly . TCFD disbanded in 2023 ; the ISSB issued its nature guidance as a voluntary Practice Statement, not a standard . PAS 2060 was withdrawn in favour of ISO 14068-1 . ESG ratings still correlate at only 0.54, against 0.92 for credit ratings .

    The same philanthropic pool funds both the accounting rule and the target validator: the Bezos Earth Fund gave the GHG Protocol $9.25m and also funds SBTi . No interference has been shown, but the structure allows it.

    Net-zero alliances needed competitors to coordinate, which is exactly the conduct antitrust law polices. They swung between toothless and legally exposed, and mostly chose toothless.

    Why they left

    The stated reasons were bland. The documented drivers were a coordinated US legal campaign, from state attorneys general to House Judiciary’s “climate cartel” reports to the FTC/DOJ statement backing Texas v. BlackRock ; commercial incentives, with bank fossil financing up 8% to $906bn in 2025 ; and physical reality, from Walmart’s refrigeration and trucking to AI load growth . Vanguard’s $29.5m settlement now formalises the toothless option .

    Targets held on paper and eroded underneath

    64% of 1,957 large listed firms still have net-zero targets, but 20 of the 43 top-50 firms with targets are backtracking, and only 11% of target-holders meet all seven integrity criteria . “ESG” in S&P 100 report titles fell from 40% to 6% ; environmental and social shareholder proposals fell about 50% between 2024 and 2026 . EU target coverage rose to 85% : the retreat is mostly American and financial.

    Chapter 6

    Carbon markets got smaller in dollars and larger in gates

    The voluntary market’s value collapsed while its tonnage held. Over-crediting is established; remediation is partial; and every new integrity layer is also a new bottleneck.

    For scale: compliance carbon-pricing revenue exceeded $107bn in 2025 , roughly 75–200 times the voluntary market’s $0.5–1.4bn . Retirements held at about 180–200 Mt a year, reaching 202 Mt in 2025 .

    The over-crediting evidence

    A synthesis covering about 1bn tonnes found under 16% of credits represent real reductions . REDD+ projects offset about three times their actual effect (Verra disputes this ); cookstoves are over-credited 9.2-fold ; at least 52% of Indian wind offsets went to projects likely built anyway .

    New issuance did tighten: Verra’s new REDD+ method cuts volumes by about 40% on a preliminary sample , and the ICVCM rejected grid renewables . But legacy stock stays tradable. At Kariba, Verra found 57% of about 27M credits issued in excess, yet buyers’ retirements “remain valid” . Two ICVCM expert advisers resigned when it labelled REDD+ methods in 2024 , and the UN mechanism approved grid renewables in July 2026, two years after the ICVCM rejected them .

    Scope 2 figures to produce

      Cheap certificates, contested meaning Scope 2

      European Guarantees of Origin traded at €0.57–0.93/MWh , around 1% of wholesale power prices and well under 1% of retail. In RE100’s 2025 disclosure members claimed 59% renewable electricity; RE100 recognised 43% . Peer-reviewed work found certificates overstated Scope 2 reductions about threefold .

      For data centres the gap between location- and market-based figures is the central fact: in 2025 location-based Scope 2 rose 29% at Google and 15% at Microsoft . Microsoft’s total rose 25% to 20.29 Mt, partly because it stopped buying unbundled certificates, which took market-based Scope 2 from about 258 kt to about 2.7 Mt .

      The GHG Protocol’s fix met its own constituency: only 12% of companies backed hourly matching and 19% deliverability . Its standards board moved to two methods: Method 1 (hourly, deliverable, incremental) companies “should” use, and Method 2 (the 2015 rules) they “may” keep until an undetermined date . SBTi V2 sidestepped the fight: emissions targets use location-based figures, and certificates count toward a separate low-carbon-electricity target where annual matching is allowed .

      The lobbying map, and who won

      Two corporate blocs fought. The hourly camp: Google, Microsoft and LevelTen through the Granular Certificate Trading Alliance . The flexibility camp: the Emissions First Partnership, co-founded by Amazon and Meta, which promotes consequential “emissions-impact” accounting , plus the April 2026 public statement by 48–60+ companies including Apple, Amazon, BYD, Salesforce, Mars and Schneider Electric . InfluenceMap found the big tech companies absent from the formal consultation responses, and more than half of 1,072 responses anonymous .

      The flexibility camp won the inventory, because Method 2 survives. The consequential camp won a venue: a proposed “impact statement” under the market-instruments work . Registries win either way.

      Scope 3: book-and-claim has outrun the accounting SAF, steel, shipping, biomethane

      Three layers disagree. The GHG Protocol says market-based approaches for SAF, biomethane and steel “are not included in or allowed under” its Corporate or Scope 3 Standard ; its market-instruments consultation proposes separate statements, backed by 84% of respondents . SBTi V2 recognises commodity certificates: mass balance within a sourced pool can sit inside the inventory, while book-and-claim supports only a “system contribution” claim, per vendor summaries . Registries already split units: one RSB unit carries one Scope 1 claim and up to two Scope 3 claims .

      Volumes are small: IATA expects about 2.4 Mt of SAF in 2026, 0.8% of airline fuel . Steel book-and-claim has no independent certification framework yet , and the IMO’s shipping framework faces another adoption attempt in December 2026 .

      A single SAF tonne can appear validly in four places: an airline’s CORSIA or EU ETS claim, a forwarder’s Scope 3, a corporate’s Scope 3 “contribution”, and a supplier’s marketing (inference) .

      Insetting and activity pools reductions claimed ahead of the rules

      PepsiCo’s 2025 climate statement appears to use certificates to lower Scope 3 by about 840,000 tCO2e; the company called the figures “directionally correct” . Nestlé books 3.31 Mt of removals within its net figures “pending SBTi guidance” . The Value Change Initiative convenes 100+ companies on market-based instruments ; its portal footer suggests SustainCERT operates it, though the site does not say so . Verra’s Scope 3 Standard Program launched on 15 September 2026, naming SustainCERT among its collaborators; version 1 lists projects but issues no units yet .

      Nature, removals, plastic more rulebooks than money

      Bloom Labs counts 58 potential national credit markets ; the Biodiversity Credit Alliance argues against a single fungible unit . England’s statutory biodiversity net gain extended to major infrastructure on 2 November 2026, but registration “does not show” outcomes delivered . Fern documented clear-cuts at an EU nature-credit pilot site in Estonia ◆advocacy source.

      The EU’s first removal-certification method entered into force on 7 May 2026, but Öko-Institut judges its integrity below the UN mechanism’s . Plastic credits fare worst: 86% of one registry’s credits came from co-processing or incineration . When carbon and biodiversity credits come from the same hectare, only carbon has an international anti-double-counting rule.

      Legal and accounting status who owns a credit, and is it an asset?

      FASB’s ASU 2026-02, issued 19 May 2026, creates Topic 818: credits held for voluntary retirement are expensed, compliance credits carried at cost, and habitat, wetland and stream credits fall outside it; it applies to public companies for years starting after 15 December 2027 . UNIDROIT’s draft treats carbon credits as property but excludes biodiversity and plastic credits . The IASB project sits on its reserve list .

      Claims law is now the binding constraint: the EU ban on offset-based neutrality claims , UK fines up to 10% of turnover , Australia’s ACCC guidance , and the Delta case, where damages claims were allowed to proceed on 28 July 2026 . The legal question is shifting from “is the credit real?” to “does the wording match what the instrument supports?”

      Chapter 8

      The instrument catalogue

      Every priced or tradable environmental unit we could find, and the rules that decide what each one can claim: 254 cards across ten families, from carbon taxes and allowances to fuel credits, EPR certificates, wetland banks, exchanges and claims law.

      How to read a card

      Each card answers the same questions: who issues the unit, how big the market is, what it can be used to claim, where it stacks with other units, what the integrity evidence says, and who captures the value. Open a card for the detail and its sources.

      The pattern across all ten families: the same physical event is monetised in several ledgers, fees settle on the mandatory nodes (exchanges, registries, producer responsibility organisations), and losses settle on treasuries, public guarantors and consumers.       Cui bono

      Both “more complexity” and “less regulation” are outcomes that particular interests worked for. Switch the view.

      The common pattern: fees track volume while losses are pooled. Registries, verifiers and raters are paid whether or not a tonne is real; over-crediting losses go to buffer pools, while the profits stayed private. Kariba earned more than $100m .

      Chapter 10

      Eight ways to explain it

      The critics agree on the diagnosis and split on the remedy. Each explanation below has real evidence for it and real evidence against.

      The weight of evidence supports a combined reading: measurement works where it is narrow, physical and priced (Scope 1 at large emitters, the EU ETS, CBAM’s threshold) and degrades as it extends into value-chain estimation, contractual attribution and claims, which is exactly where 2024–26 rule-making and lobbying concentrated.

      Chapter 11

      So, is it unsustainable?

      Four tests. Make your own call before you reveal the answer.

      Hollowing, not collapse

      The system is unsustainable as an integrated claims architecture: the promise that one inventory could flow through law, targets, markets and marketing has failed, and the 2026 settlement formally swaps it for separate ledgers. It remains resilient as an industry and as a set of narrower physical regimes, with more than $107bn in compliance carbon-pricing revenue, CBAM live, and 64% of large firms still holding net-zero targets .

      For a verifier, assurance shifts from “is this number right?” to “is this number labelled with the right method, ledger and claim?” That work scales with the number of ledgers rather than with emissions.
